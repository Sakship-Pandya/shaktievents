## Signing in

**Screen:** `/admin/login`

Your website address with /admin at the end. Save it in your phone.

1. Type your email and password and tap Sign in.
2. If you get the password wrong five times, the door shuts for fifteen minutes. That is on purpose — it stops anyone guessing their way in. Wait and try again.

> Never share your password. If somebody else needs to get in, give them their own account — Users & roles, further down this page.

## Enquiries — do this every day

**Screen:** `/admin/enquiries`

Every quote request from the website lands here, and a copy comes to your email. This is the only screen you must open daily.

1. Open the enquiry. You get the name, the mobile number, the town, the date, roughly how many guests and what they want set up.
2. Ring them. Then move the enquiry along: Contacted, Site visit, Quoted, Booked, or Lost.
3. Write what happened in the notes. Six months later, when they ring back, that note is the whole conversation.

> Marking an enquiry Lost is not a failure — it keeps the list honest, so what is still open is genuinely still open.

> The Export button gives you the whole list as a spreadsheet, for whoever does your accounts.

## Adding a function you have done

**Screen:** `/admin/projects`

This is the most valuable half hour you can spend on the website. Photographs of real work is what makes somebody ring you instead of the next contractor.

1. Projects › New project. Give it a title in both languages — say what it was and where: “Wedding mandap at Limbdi”.
2. Fill in the venue, the date, the number of guests and how many days you were on site. Those numbers are what a visitor is really judging.
3. Tick the services you used, and choose the kind of function and the town. That is what puts this job on the right service page and the right town page.
4. Add the photographs. One cover photo, then as many gallery photos as you have. A before-and-after pair is worth ten ordinary photos.
5. Set the status to Published and save. It appears on the site within a minute.

> Leave it as Draft while you are still gathering photographs. Nobody sees a draft.

> A client quote goes on the project only with that family’s permission. Ask before you type it.

## Photographs

**Screen:** `/admin/media`

Every photograph on the site lives here. Upload once, use it anywhere.

1. Drag photographs in, or tap Upload. Straight from the phone is fine — the site shrinks them for you.
2. Write a one-line description of each photograph in both languages. It is what a blind visitor hears and what Google reads. “Red and gold mandap at Wadhwan” — not “DSC_4471”.

> Landscape photographs work better than portrait everywhere on this site.

> Do not upload a photograph you did not take, and do not upload a stock picture of somebody else’s mandap. The whole point is that this is your work.

## Services

**Screen:** `/admin/services`

The fourteen things you set up, each with its own page. These pages are what bring people in from Google.

1. Open a service to change its description, what is included, the sizes you carry, and the questions people ask about it.
2. Check the specification tables against your own equipment. They were written as a starting point, not from your godown.

> Do not change the web address of a service once Google knows it. Turn a service off instead of deleting it.

## Towns

**Screen:** `/admin/areas`

One page for each town you serve — Limbdi, Chotila, Muli and the rest. Somebody searching “mandap Limbdi” lands on that page.

1. Add the halls, wadis and grounds you have actually worked at in that town. That list is the one thing a stranger cannot fake and Google cannot ignore.
2. Add the travel time from Surendranagar and anything true about that town — the wedding season there, the size of the grounds, how the approach road is.

## Packages and prices

**Screen:** `/admin/packages`

The four ready-made combinations. Prices are blank until you fill them in, and blank is safer than wrong.

> A price on the website is a promise. Put a starting figure only if you will honour it, and change it the day your rates change.

## Reviews

**Screen:** `/admin/testimonials`

Real reviews from real families. The page is empty until you add some, and it says so honestly.

1. Ask a happy family for two lines on WhatsApp. Ask if you may put it on the website. Then type it here with their name and town.

> Never write a review yourself. A made-up review is the fastest way to lose the trust the rest of the site is built on — and Google is good at spotting them.

## Guides

**Screen:** `/admin/blog`

Eight articles answering what people actually ask before they book. They bring in visitors who are not yet ready to ring.

1. Read each one and correct anything that is not true of your business — especially the numbers.
2. To write a new one: New post, write in the editor, add a cover photograph, then Publish.

## The home page

**Screen:** `/admin/sections`

The home page is built from blocks you can reorder, hide and adjust. Pages & sections is where you do it.

1. Drag a block up or down to move it. Tap the eye to hide it. Tap the block to change its settings.

> Hiding a block is not deleting it. Bring it back whenever you like.

## The strip at the top

**Screen:** `/admin/announcements`

The thin bar above the menu. Use it for one thing at a time: dates filling up, a festival offer, a phone number that changed.

> Switch it off when it stops being true. A bar still advertising last season makes the whole site look abandoned.

## Business details

**Screen:** `/admin/settings`

Your name, address, phone, WhatsApp and email. These appear in the footer, on the contact page, in every enquiry email and in what Google reads.

1. Fill in everything showing [TBD]. Until you do, the site shows [TBD] to visitors rather than inventing something.

> Write the address exactly the same way here, on Google Business Profile and on JustDial. Different versions of the same address is the commonest reason a local business ranks below its neighbour.

## The quote estimator

**Screen:** `/admin/settings/estimator`

What the quote form uses to show a rough range. Until you set base prices it shows no figure at all and tells the visitor a quote follows a site visit — which is the safe default, not a fault.

> Set the base prices low. A visitor who is told ₹40,000 and then quoted ₹90,000 does not book; a visitor told ₹40,000 and quoted ₹45,000 does.

## Extra features

**Screen:** `/admin/settings/features`

Five optional additions, all switched off. Turn one on, look at the site, turn it off again if you do not like it. Anything switched off is not sent to the visitor at all, so it costs your visitors nothing.

> The muhurat countdown needs dates. Get them from whoever the family uses for the panchang — nobody has filled them in, because the wrong dates would be worse than none.

> The popup brings enquiries and it also irritates people. If you turn it on, watch the enquiry count for two weeks either side. If it does not move, turn it off.

## Who else can sign in

**Screen:** `/admin/users`

Give each person their own account. Sharing one login means the record of who changed what is worthless — and that record is the only way to work out what went wrong later.

1. Add somebody: their name, their email, and whether they are an Editor or an Admin.
2. An Editor can change everything on the public site. An Admin can also add people and see the audit log. Most people should be Editors.
3. The password is generated and shown to you once. Write it down and give it to them in person or by SMS — not on WhatsApp. It cannot be shown again; if it is lost, tap New password.
4. When somebody leaves, switch their account off. That stops them signing in and keeps their name on the work they did.

> Removing an account for good is there for one thing only: an email typed wrongly when the account was made. For everybody else, switch off instead.

> You cannot switch off your own account, and the last admin cannot be switched off or demoted. That is on purpose — otherwise nobody would be left who could switch it back.

## Who changed what

**Screen:** `/admin/audit`

Every change anybody makes through this panel is written down automatically — who, what, when. Nobody can edit it, including you.

> Use it when something on the site is not what you expected. Filter by what was changed — Project, Service, Settings — and the answer is usually in the top few lines.

## Four rules worth keeping

The website was built on these. Break them and it slowly stops working.

1. Never write something you cannot stand behind. No made-up review, no number of functions you have not done, no price you will not honour.
2. Keep your name, address and phone identical everywhere — this site, Google, JustDial, your visiting card.
3. Add one function a month with photographs. That habit alone will do more for the site than anything else on this page.
4. Fill in both languages every time. Half the district searches in Gujarati.

## If something looks wrong

In order, these fix almost everything.

1. Wait a minute and reload. Pages are stored ready-made for speed; a change takes up to a minute to appear.
2. Check you saved. The Save button turns and says so when it worked.
3. Check you filled in both languages. A missing Gujarati line shows as a blank on the Gujarati page.
4. Change it back to what it was. Nothing here is permanent.
